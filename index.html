5 · Self-employment: the business pays for the benefits

A fictional freelance worker receives $5,500 in monthly client payments but spends $1,400 on business expenses, $450 on health coverage, and sets aside $350 for retirement.

StepCalculation / estimate

Illustrative tax reserve: 25% of $4,100 business profit (a planning assumption, not an actual tax rate)
Cash left after this illustrative tax set-aside, health coverage, and retirement
Who withholds taxes? Employers usually withhold income and payroll taxes from employee paychecks. A self-employed freelancer generally receives client payments without tax withholding and must plan to pay income tax and self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare), often through quarterly estimated payments. The 25% reserve in this activity is only a math example, not a recommended rate or a calculation of anyone's actual taxes. The tax obligation depends on the worker's circumstances. IRS: self-employed tax guidance.

This is not an exact take-home-pay or tax calculation. Business profit, spending cash, and taxable income are not interchangeable. Unpaid time off, business risk, and insurance coverage also matter.
