4 · Three job offers: compare, calculate, decide

These fictional offers are for different jobs a worker could reasonably consider. Pay and benefits are illustrative. First calculate, then match offers to people.

A · Ridgeway Services

$60,000 annual salary
  • Employee health insurance premiums: $3,600/year
  • Employer retirement contribution: $0
  • Paid time off: 10 days
  • Schedule: fixed hours, on-site
  • Disability coverage: not included
  • Professional training: limited

B · Summit Community Partners

$54,000 annual salary
  • Employee health insurance premiums: $1,200/year
  • Automatic employer retirement contribution: 4% ($2,160/year); eligibility assumed
  • Paid time off: 20 days
  • Schedule: hybrid
  • Disability coverage: included
  • Professional training: paid by employer

C · Juniper Table, Aurora

Restaurant server: $12.14/hour + tips
  • Hours fluctuate: 25–40 hours/week
  • Slow week: 25 hours + $90 in tips
  • Midrange example: 32 hours + $300 in tips = about $33,047 gross/year (48 weeks)
  • Busy week: 40 hours + $600 in tips
  • Assume 48 working weeks; four weeks off are unpaid
  • No employer health plan or retirement contribution in this fictional offer
  • Hours and tips vary; none of these earnings are guaranteed

Important: Offer C's displayed $33,047 is an illustrative middle-income scenario, not a promised salary, median wage, or a calculation students have completed. You will calculate slow- and busy-week estimates below using 48 paid working weeks (four weeks off without pay). Contrast that with the paid time off available in Offers A and B. Salary minus employee premiums is NOT take-home pay; PTO is not extra salary cash.

Five people, five decisions

Choose A, B, or C for each person. Explain with one specific number and a nonmonetary tradeoff (one sentence or two per person). There can be more than one defensible answer.

1. Parker has regular medical visits and values predictable healthcare premiums.
2. Alexis wants paid time to earn a credential and is willing to accept lower immediate salary.
3. Jordan urgently needs the most dependable base earnings and prefers a fixed workplace schedule.
4. Skyler values schedule flexibility and free time, and can tolerate a lower initial salary.
5. Riley enjoys restaurant work but needs to know whether tips and variable shifts support a stable budget.
STOP · Each group defends a different decision using math AND context. No single offer is always best.

Self-employment: Who pays for the benefits?

A fictional freelance worker receives $5,500 in monthly client payments but spends $1,400 on business expenses, $450 on health coverage, and sets aside $350 for retirement.

StepCalculation / estimate
Business profit before personal costs and taxes
Remaining after health coverage and retirement set-aside (BEFORE taxes)

Not take-home pay: taxes, unpaid time off, business risk, and coverage limits are not fully modeled. Client payments are revenue, not profit; self-employed workers may arrange their own health and disability coverage, retirement savings, and tax payments.
